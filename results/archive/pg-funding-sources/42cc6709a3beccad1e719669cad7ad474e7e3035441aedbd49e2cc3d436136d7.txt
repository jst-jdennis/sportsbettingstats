March 31, 2025 - Updated December 17, 2025
To: The Honorable Kathy Hochul
Members of the New York State Legislature
On behalf of the New York State Gaming Commission and the Office of Addiction Services and
Supports (OASAS), we respectfully submit this annual report as required by N.Y. Racing, Pari-
Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law Section 1367-a(6). The statute requires the Commission and
OASAS to submit an annual report to the Governor and Legislature “on the impact of mobile
sports wagering on problem gamblers in New York, including, to the extent practicable, an
analysis of demographics which are disproportionately impacted by the problem gambling.”
The 2024 report is the first to include specific data points from the nine licensed mobile sports
wagering operators in New York State. The regulation addressing such was adopted in April
2025, meaning operators were not required to record such datapoints in 2024. Regardless, the
Commission provided a template worksheet to all operators with instruction and guidance to
provide existing data. The Commission and OASAS spent significant time processing the millions
of datapoints submitted in response and working with operators to obtain comparable datasets.
Inconsistent and incompatible data submissions led to delays in completing this report. Optional
patron-provided demographic data was not yet required to be maintained by operators in 2024
and therefore is not included in this report.
However, this report provides baseline statistics on general account and wager information on
which to build future evaluation and research.
The Commission and OASAS will continue to partner to research, analyze and address
demographic statistics regarding mobile sports wagering to ensure that resources are best
directed where they are needed, and that protections are put in place to protect vulnerable New
Yorkers.
Respectfully Submitted,
Robert Williams Chinazo Cunningham, M.D., M.S.

N.Y. Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367-a(6)
The commission, in conjunction with the office of addiction services and supports, shall annually
cause a report to be prepared and distributed to the governor and the legislature on the impact
of mobile sports wagering on problem gamblers in New York, including, to the extent
practicable, an analysis of demographics which are disproportionately impacted by the problem
gambling. The costs associated with the preparation and distribution of the report shall be borne
by mobile sports wagering licensees and the commission shall be authorized to assess a fee
against mobile sports wagering licensees for these purposes. The commission, or in the case
that an independent integrity monitor has been established, such independent integrity monitor
shall also report biannually to the governor and the legislature on the effectiveness of the
statutory and regulatory controls in place to ensure the integrity of mobile sports wagering
operations.
N.Y. Mental Hygiene Law § 19.07(a)
The office of alcoholism and substance abuse services is charged with the responsibility for
assuring the development of comprehensive plans, programs, and services in the areas of
research, prevention, care, treatment, rehabilitation, including relapse prevention and recovery
maintenance, education, and training of persons who abuse or are dependent on alcohol and/or
substances and their families. Such plans, programs, and services shall be developed with the
cooperation of the office, the other offices of the department where appropriate, local
governments, consumers and community organizations and entities. The office shall provide
appropriate facilities and shall encourage the provision of facilities by local government and
community organizations and entities. The office is also responsible for developing plans,
programs and services related to compulsive gambling education, prevention and treatment
consistent with section 41.57 of this chapter.

Background
Chapter 59 of the Laws of 2021 amended N.Y. Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law
§ 1367 and added § 1367-a to authorize mobile sports wagering when the sports wager is made
through virtual or electronic means from a location within New York State (NYS) and is
transmitted to and accepted by electronic equipment located at a licensed commercial casino
gaming facility. The statute also directed the Gaming Commission to conduct a competitive
bidding process to award mobile sports wagering licenses. The Commission unanimously
accepted the review committee’s recommendations and awarded nine platform providers and
operators with 10-year licenses at a tax rate of 51 percent.
The first mobile sports wager in NYS was placed January 8, 2022. Since then, the Commission
has enacted additional regulatory measures to protect the betting public, including but not
limited to the prohibition of mobile sports wagering marketing and advertising on college
campuses and ensuring all operators understand New York’s requirement that a gambling harms
message be included on all advertising,
OASAS oversees a continuum of care for addiction services that includes prevention, treatment,
harm reduction and recovery support. Access to both inpatient and outpatient services for those
affected by harms related to gambling, including for those who have been diagnosed with
gambling disorder, are available throughout the state. Further, there are a range of community-
based prevention efforts to raise the awareness the prevention and risk reduction of gambling.
Further, OASAS seeks to mitigate potential harms associated with gambling by promoting harm
reduction strategies such as setting additional time and money wagered limits, and limiting
alcohol and drug use while gambling. In 2025, OASAS released a statewide public awareness
campaign, Take a Pause, targeting those that are participating in mobile sports wagering and
encouraging them to take a short survey that provides normative feedback on their current
gambling behaviors and tips and resources if needed.

Revenue for Gambling Harms Treatment and Education
Currently, $6 million annually from New York State’s tax on mobile sports wagering is dedicated
for gambling harms education and treatment purposes, pursuant to N.Y. Racing, Pari-Mutuel
Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367(8). Beginning in 2026, an additional $6 million annually is
directed for such purposes.
In addition to revenue from mobile sports wagering operators, existing commercial casino
operators, of which there are currently four, pay an annual fee of $500 per slot machine and
table to New York State for OASAS-directed gambling harms treatment and education. By
statute, any additional commercial casino sited in New York must pay a $750 annual fee per slot
machine and table to New York State for gambling harms treatment and education.
Marketing and Advertising Regulations
New York State’s mobile sports wagering regulations:
• Hold licensees responsible for the content of their advertising, marketing and branding;
• Require all public-facing advertisements to clearly include the HOPEline to increase
awareness and availability of services for those affected by gambling harms;
• Prohibit false, deceptive, or misleading statements;
• Require that material facts and terms and conditions in all marketing and promotions be
clearly and conspicuously disclosed;
• Allow customers to opt-out of receiving future direct advertisements;
• Prohibit advertisements to underage persons or design elements that appeal primarily to
underage persons; and
• Prohibit mobile sports wagering from being promoted or advertised in college or
university-owned news assets such as school newspapers, radio, or television broadcasts.
The Commission’s Gaming Division and Compliance unit review all operators’ proposed
marketing and advertising materials to ensure they comply with these regulations.

Sports Wagering Regulation
Mobile sports wagering is codified in New York State Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding
Law § 1367. The Commission’s regulation of mobile sports wagering is covered by section 5330.
Geolocation
New York State law defines an “authorized sports bettor” as “an individual who is physically
present in this state when placing a sports wager…” Part 5330 of the Commission’s regulations
require licensed operators to obtain Commission-approved geolocation services to ensure that
all wagering account activity takes place within NYS’ borders (§ 5330.44).

Account-Related Information on Mobile Sports Wagering Platforms
A range of account-related information is provided to authorized sports bettors on the mobile
sport wagering platforms, including deposit limits, time limits, total wager limits, maximum, and
loss limits. The availability of these self-imposed responsible gaming limits within each mobile
sports operator’s website or mobile application are required by Commission Rule 5330.34.
In 2024, all mobile sport wagering operators offered at least one of these account-related
information notification points. Among the nine sports wagering operators in NYS, 89 percent
offered total wager limit information, 78 percent offered time limit information, 67 percent offered
maximum wager size limit information, and 44 percent offered loss limit information.

Underage Access
New York State Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367-a(4)(a)(iii) obligates each
mobile sports wagering operator to implement measures to prohibit minors from participating in
any sports wagering activity. The Commission, OASAS and the New York Council on Problem
Gambling launched new training modules for retail and gaming facility employees to recognize
and prevent underage access in 2024.
Additionally, as part of Commission Rule 5325.2(b)(3)(x) and New York State Racing, Pari-Mutuel
Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367-a(4)(a)(xv), applied through Commission Rules 5329.34 and
5330.34, each operator was obligated to submit for approval, a problem-gaming plan that
includes procedures to prevent gaming by minors.
Through the end of 2024, the Commission was informed of 1,871 alleged occasions of underaged
participation through lawfully created accounts. All verified underage access was due to lawfully
established accounts created by of-age individuals providing access to and play by underage
individuals. In all circumstances, the subject account was closed.
This is a marked increase in underage attempts from 2023, when the Commission was informed
of 54 such occasions. The increase is partly attributable to operators enhancing existing know-
your-customer measures to better detect attempts at underage play.

Voluntary Self-Exclusion
New York State has a robust voluntary self-exclusion (VSE) program for those individuals who
recognize that they should no longer participate in legal gambling. An individual can self-exclude
from all legal gambling statewide for one year, three years, five years or for their lifetime. This
program is unique in that it prohibits participation in all legal gambling options regulated by the
Commission, including mobile sports wagering.
Between January 1, 2017, and December 31, 2024, 8,200 individuals signed up for self-exclusion.
In 2024, there were 4,541 active individuals on the self-exclusion list.
HOPEline
OASAS operates a multi-lingual 24/7 confidential HOPEline (1-877-846-7369) which provides on-
demand crisis support and referrals for those experiencing gambling harms and/or substance use
harms. Calls are answered by bachelor’s- and master’s-level clinicians who have training in crisis
response and who have extensive knowledge of gambling and substance use harms. Callers
receive referrals as needed including to community-based supports as well as warm transfers to
one of seven Problem Gambling Resource Centers throughout the State. Further, all callers have
the option to talk to a peer with lived experience.
Between January 1, 2020 and December 31, 2024, the volume of calls to the HOPEline increased
30.7 percent from 2,345 calls in 2020 to 3,064 calls in 2024. The increase coincides with
additional exposure and access to the HOPEline through public service announcements,
gambling operator advertisements, and other promotional efforts to highlight the line as a
resource for those experiencing gambling harms.
Prior to mobile sports wagering launching in January 2022, the top reason for calls made by
those experiencing gambling harms were harms related to casino-based gambling. Since January
2022, the top reason has been online betting (e.g. mobile sports wagering).
In 2024, the demographic group contacting the HOPEline the most frequently was males
between the ages of 25-44.
Looking Forward
The practical implementation of the Commission’s regulations regarding mobile sports wagering
data collection becomes effective February 28, 2026. At that time, operators are expected to
provide – on a template provided by the Commission and OASAS – datasets and demographics
for calendar year 2025. OASAS and the Commission will promptly compile and analyze the data
against the 2024 baseline information provided in this report.
Collectively, the Commission and OASAS will continue their collaborative work to curb gambling-
related harms and ensure help is available for those who need it. The agencies will also make
improvements as needed to policies and regulations as new data and circumstances warrant.
