Executive This report details the State Fiscal Year
2024 (SFY24) expenditures for Vermont’s
Summary
Responsible Gaming Program, managed
by the Department of Mental Health
(DMH). The program, aimed at
establishing and maintaining responsible
gaming standards, spent $34,814.09 of
$250,000 of available funds from a
Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)
with the Department of Liquor and Lottery
(DLL) and utilized $9,775.26 from DMH
funds.
In SFY24, DMH achieved the lead goals of
establishing a Vermont-specific problem-
gambling helpline and website and
continues to develop the Problem
Gambling Provider Network (PGPN) to
ensure training and development of a
clinical workforce to support Vermonters in
need of problem gambling treatment, with
the goal of creating a comprehensive and
accessible system to prevent and address
problem gambling in Vermont.

Expenditures for State Fiscal Year 2024
In the early part of State Fiscal Year (SFY) 2024, expenditures on
the Responsible Gaming Program activities were minimal, as the
program primarily utilized in-kind staff time. During the period, the
Department of Mental Health (DMH) staff collaborated with the
Department of Liquor and Lottery (DLL) and regional and national
colleagues to collaborate on resources for Vermonters (for more
information on the challenges associated with program
implementation see the 2024 Problem Gambling Program &
Expenditures Report). Due to the availability of funds from the
MOU with DLL, the program did not draw from the $250,000 in
funding available from the Sports Wagering Enterprise Fund.
On December 4th, 2023, DMH issued a Request for Proposals to
identify a vendor to support the program in meeting the state’s
responsible gaming and problem gambling program. The
Massachusetts Council on Gaming and Health (MACGH) was
selected as the vendor, and a contract was signed on February
15th, 2024.
In SFY24, the majority of Responsible Gaming Program
expenditures in SFY24 were related to the MACGH contract, with
DMH spending $34,814.09 under the agreement. Additional funds
totaling $9,775.26 were spent on personnel costs and other
program expenses not associated with the contract.

Problem Gambling Helpline Established
In SFY24, DMH worked with the National Problem Gambling
Helpline (herein after referred to as the Helpline), MACGH, and a
core group of clinicians comprising the Vermont Problem
Gambling Provider Network, to improve overall accessibility for
Vermonters seeking support and resources for problem gambling
concerns. DMH then ensured Vermont treatment provider
information is accessible to individuals reaching out to the
Helpline at 1-800-GAMBLER, a nationally recognized helpline
which covers all 50 states and U.S. territories.
Prior to the establishment of the Responsible Gaming Program at
DMH, when a caller with an 802 area code or who self-identified
as a Vermonter reached the Helpline at 1-800-522-4700 or 1-800-
GAMBLER, they received non-Vermont-specific services. If a
Vermont caller requested a referral for local treatment services,
they were directed to the Howard Center in Burlington. From
SFY19 through SFY22, the Howard Center provided no-cost
gambling services statewide through a contract with the
Department of Liquor and Lottery. After this contract ended,
Howard Center remained the referral source on record for the
Helpline.
In December 2023, DMH staff worked with leadership at the
Helpline to expand referral options for individuals seeking support
for problem gambling assistance in Vermont, by using the
Vermont Problem Gambling Provider Network in addition to the
existing Howard Center clinician.
In January 2024, MACGH took over management of Vermont-
based calls from the Helpline. Trained peer Helpline Specialists
employed by MACGH answer calls 24/7, 365 days of the year and
have access to information on the Problem Gambling Provider

DMH and DLL’s use of the Helpline number, 1-800-GAMBLER,
increases visibility and accessibility for Vermonters to reach
skilled and trained Helpline Specialists available to respond to
calls 24/7/365. This number is used nationally on mobile sports

The previous Helpline number, 1-800-522-4700, is currently listed

The establishment and ongoing management of this helpline

investment in enhancing accessibility and support for problem
gambling resources in Vermont.
Peer Specialist Support
MACGH has been training MACGH peer specialist staff to provide
support to Vermonters who are offered and agree to engage with
peer specialists for Telephone Recovery Support program. This
program is designed to assist individuals after either opting out of
online sports wagering apps via the state-run Voluntary Self-
Exclusion program or seeking support through the Problem
Gambling Helpline.
The Telephone Recovery Support Program will provide
personalized peer supports tailored to the needs of the individual
navigating recovery. DMH anticipates this service will be offered
to Vermonters beginning in SFY25.

Vermont Gambling Help Website
In June 2024, DMH partnered with MACGH to launch
VtGamblingHelp.org, a comprehensive website for Vermonters
affected by problem gambling. The site provides resources for
individuals and families, as well as treatment providers seeking to
better assist those in need.
A key component of VtGamblingHelp.org is the LiveChat feature,
which connects users directly with Vermont Helpline Specialists
from the 1-800-GAMBLER service through web-based or phone-
based text chatting. By offering multiple ways Vermonters can
access support, DMH ensures Vermonters can choose the
method that best fits their needs, whether by phone or chat.
DMH will be monitoring the total chats per quarter, in addition to
general chat topics and will be able to report on this information in
SFY25 and subsequent SFYs. This monitoring will help evaluate
the effectiveness of the LiveChat feature and guide future
iterations and improvements.
The development and maintenance of the website cost $2,398.22
in SFY24, reflecting a critical investment in expanding outreach
and access to problem gambling resources in Vermont.

Problem Gambling Clinical Training and Certificate Program
In the fall of 2023, DMH supported Designated Agencies (DAs) in
identifying and providing clinicians with access to free problem
gambling training through the New York Council on Problem
Gambling. Eleven clinicians from nine DAs committed to engaging
in training throughout the year and attended Problem Gambling
Provider Network meetings quarterly. At the close of SFY24, the
Problem Gambling Provider Network had clinicians from nine DAs
in attendance at meetings and is working to have a fully trained
DA clinician for each catchment area. In addition, the Problem
Gambling Provider Network contains and continues to recruit
private clinicians across the state who are trained or are willing to
become trained problem gambling clinicians.
To support the continued development of Vermont’s problem
gambling workforce, DMH partnered with MACGH to craft a
Vermont-specific training program. This program will be offered
twice in SFY25, at no cost to clinicians in Vermont or clinicians
who provide services to Vermonters. MACGH prepared the
curriculum for this 30-hour Problem Gambling Essentials for
Clinicians training, set to run as a Day Training Institute in
October – November 2024, and as an Evening Training Institute
in February – March 2025.
Clinicians who complete this 30-hour training, or 30 hours of
equivalent International Gambling Counselor Certification Board
approved courses, along with meeting additional criteria, are
eligible to apply for the Vermont Problem Gambling Specialist
Certificate.
This certificate will allow for placement on the Vermont Problem
Gambling Helpline Referral List and indicates that the clinician
has been well-trained in gambling disorder assessment,
treatment, and recovery support. DMH will market this opportunity

Contractor Administrative Fees
As part of the MACGH contract, an administrative fee of 10% was
also applied to all bills. In total for SFY24, administrative fees
were $3,164.91.
Responsible Gaming Program Coordinator
The Responsible Gaming Program Coordinator, hired in May
2024, manages the daily operations of the Responsible Gaming
Program, ensuring effective implementation and compliance with
Vermont Statute 31 V.S.A. § 1341a. This role includes close
collaboration with the Department of Liquor and Lottery (DLL) and
the responsible gaming and problem gambling program’s
contractor to align strategies and initiatives.

Problem Gambling Awareness Month – March
2024
On February 28th, 2024, Governor Scott declared March 2024
Problem Gambling Awareness Month. To support Problem
Gambling Awareness Month, the DMH Medical Director and
Deputy Commissioner coordinated a series of activities and
events to highlight the importance of addressing problem
gambling and promoting available resources.
Key Activities included:
Media Engagement: The DMH Medical Director appeared
•
on multiple news outlets to educate the public about the
warning signs of problem gambling and the importance of
seeking help when recreational gaming becomes
problematic.
Outreach to College Students: The Medical Director
•
attended a University of Vermont event, distributing
promotional materials to encourage students to utilize the 1-
800-GAMBLER helpline for support and resources.
Expenses for Problem Gambling Awareness Month events totaled
$297.53.
