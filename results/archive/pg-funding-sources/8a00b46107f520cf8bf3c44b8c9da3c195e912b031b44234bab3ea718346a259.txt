On behalf of the New York State Gaming Commission and the Office of Addiction Services and
Supports (OASAS), we respectfully submit this annual report as required by N.Y. Racing, Pari-
Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law Section 1367-a(6). The statute requires the Commission and

mobile sports wagering licensees in New York State. The Commission provided a template
worksheet to all licensees with instruction and guidance to provide existing datapoints. The
Commission and OASAS spent significant time processing the tens of millions of datapoints
submitted in response and working with licensees to obtain consistent datasets.
The Commission and OASAS will continue to partner to research, analyze and address
demographic statistics regarding mobile sports wagering to ensure that resources are best
directed where they are needed, and that protections are put in place to protect vulnerable New
Yorkers.

The commission, in conjunction with the office of addiction services and supports, shall annually
cause a report to be prepared and distributed to the governor and the legislature on the impact

Background
Chapter 59 of the Laws of 2021 amended N.Y. Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law
§ 1367 and added § 1367-a to authorize mobile sports wagering when the sports wager is made
through virtual or electronic means from a location within New York State and is transmitted to
and accepted by electronic equipment located at a licensed commercial casino gaming facility.
The statute also directed the Commission to conduct a competitive bidding process to award
mobile sports wagering licenses. The Commission unanimously accepted the review committee’s
recommendations and awarded nine platform providers and operators with 10-year licenses at a
tax rate of 51 percent.
The first mobile sports wager in N.Y.S. was placed January 8, 2022. Since then, the Commission
has enacted additional regulatory measures to protect the betting public, including but not
limited to the prohibition of mobile sports wagering marketing and advertising on college
campuses and ensuring all licensees understand New York’s requirement that a gambling harms
message be included on all advertising,
OASAS oversees a continuum of care for addiction services that includes prevention, treatment,
harm reduction and recovery support. Access to both inpatient and outpatient services for those
affected by harms related to gambling, including for those who have been diagnosed with
gambling disorder, are available throughout the state. Further, there are a range of community-
based prevention efforts to raise awareness of the prevention and risk reduction of gambling.
Further, OASAS seeks to mitigate potential harms associated with gambling by promoting harm
reduction strategies such as setting additional time and money wagered limits, and limiting
alcohol and drug use while gambling. In 2025, OASAS released a statewide public awareness
campaign, Take a Pause, targeting those that are participating in mobile sports wagering and
encouraging them to take a short survey that provides normative feedback on their current
gambling behaviors and tips and resources if needed.

Revenue for Gambling Harms Treatment and Education
Currently, $6 million annually from New York State’s tax on mobile sports wagering is dedicated
for gambling harms education and treatment purposes, pursuant to N.Y. Racing, Pari-Mutuel
Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367(8). Beginning in 2026, an additional $6 million annually is
directed for such purposes.

In addition to revenue from mobile sports wagering licensees, existing commercial casino

Marketing and Advertising Regulations
New York State’s mobile sports wagering regulations:
• Hold licensees responsible for the content of their advertising, marketing and branding;
• Require all public-facing advertisements to clearly include the HOPEline to increase
awareness and availability of services for those affected by gambling harms;
• Prohibit false, deceptive, or misleading statements;
• Require that material facts and terms and conditions in all marketing and promotions be
clearly and conspicuously disclosed;
• Allow customers to opt-out of receiving future direct advertisements;
• Prohibit advertisements to underage persons or design elements that appeal primarily to
underage persons; and
• Prohibit mobile sports wagering from being promoted or advertised in college or
university-owned news assets such as school newspapers, radio, or television broadcasts.
The Commission’s Division of Gaming and Bureau of Risk Management’s Compliance Unit review
all licensees’ proposed marketing and advertising materials to ensure they comply with these
regulations.

Sports Wagering Regulation
The authority to conduct mobile sports wagering is codified in N.Y.S. Racing, Pari-Mutuel
Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367-a. The Commission’s regulation of mobile sports wagering is
covered by N.Y. Comp. Codes R. & Regs. tit. 9, Part 5330.
Geolocation
New York State law defines an “authorized sports bettor” as “an individual who is physically
present in this state when placing a sports wager…” Commission Rule 5330.44 requires licensees
to obtain Commission-approved geolocation services to ensure that all wagering account activity
takes place within the State’s borders.

limits. The availability of these self-imposed responsible gaming limits within each mobile sports
licensee’s website or mobile application are required by Commission Rule 5330.34.

Underage Access
N.Y.S. Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367-a(4)(a)(iii) obligates each mobile
sports wagering operator to implement measures to prohibit minors from participating in any
sports wagering activity. The Commission, OASAS and the New York Council on Problem
Gambling launched new training modules for retail and gaming facility employees to recognize
and prevent underage access in 2025.
Additionally, as part of N.Y.S. Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law § 1367-a(4)(a)(xv)
and Commission Rule 5325.2(b)(3)(x), applied through Commission Rules 5329.34 and 5330.34,
each operator was obligated to submit for approval, a problem-gambling plan that includes
procedures to prevent gaming by minors.
This year, Commission staff requested each licensee report data regarding underage gambling
activity. Likely due to inconsistent reporting methodologies, only three licensees identified such
activity. Standardization of reporting will be sought for 2026 data.

One mobile sports wagering licensee reported 785 occasions wherein underaged individuals
purportedly commenced the account establishment process. The licensee, which also operates a
licensed Interactive Fantasy Sports platform, advised these accounts were initially verified
through KYC procedures. However, prior to depositing funds or placing a wager, the applicants
were required to submit further age identification. Those who were underage for mobile sports
wagering had their accounts restricted and disabled from mobile sports wagering capability, but
remained active for Interactive Fantasy Sports utilization, which has a lower age of participation.
Another licensee identified four account applicants below the age of 21 who accepted the
platform’s terms and conditions and spent time on the platform. However, when such underaged
individuals sought to further engage with the licensee, they were directed to a screen identifying
them as underaged. In no circumstances were any underage applicants permitted to make a
deposit or sports wager.
A third operator identified two accounts opened in the last quarter of 2024 by underaged people.
Each account was funded and utilized by the account holders through mid-March 2025, at which
point they were identified by routine KYC testing as suspect and closed. While the manner of
detection avoidance has been identified and remedied, these incidents are under review by the
Commission and in stages of administrative adjudication.
In last year’s Annual Report, the Commission noted 1,871 incidences involving underage
gambling, with many being parents or friends providing unauthorized access to their lawfully
established accounts by underaged family members or friends. In calendar 2025, the number of
reported underage incidences was 1,972, again primarily due to lawfully established accounts
created by of-age individuals providing access to and play by underage individuals.
Voluntary Self-Exclusion
New York State has a robust voluntary self-exclusion program for those individuals who
recognize that they should no longer participate in legal gambling. An individual can self-exclude
from all legal gambling statewide for one year, three years, five years or for their lifetime.
Between January 1, 2017, and December 31, 2025, 12,404 individuals signed up for self-exclusion.
In 2025, there were 1,887 active individuals on the self-exclusion list.
HOPEline
OASAS operates a multi-lingual 24/7/365 confidential HOPEline (1-877-846-7369) which provides
on-demand crisis support and referrals for those experiencing gambling harms and/or substance
use harms. Calls are answered by bachelor’s- and master’s-level clinicians who have training in
crisis response and who have extensive knowledge of gambling and substance use harms.
Callers receive referrals as needed including to community-based supports as well as warm
transfers to one of seven Problem Gambling Resource Centers throughout the State. Further, all
callers have the option to talk to a peer with lived experience.
Between January 1, 2020 and December 31, 2025, the volume of calls to the HOPEline increased
8.5 percent from 2,345 calls in 2020 to 2,545 calls in 2025. The increase coincides with

Looking Forward
The practical implementation of the Commission’s regulation regarding mobile sports wagering
data collection became effective February 28, 2026. Licensees were expected to provide – on a
template provided by the Commission and OASAS – datasets and demographics for calendar
year 2025.
Collectively, the Commission and OASAS will continue their collaborative work to curb gambling-
related harms and ensure help is available for those who need it. The agencies will also make
improvements as needed to policies and regulations and collection protocols as new data and
circumstances warrant.
