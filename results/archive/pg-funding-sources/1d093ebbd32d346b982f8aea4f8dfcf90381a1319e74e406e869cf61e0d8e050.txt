HONORABLE EXECUTIVE OFFICERS AND MEMBERS OF THE GREAT AND GENERAL COURT,
Fiscal Year 2025 (FY2025) was another exciting year for the Massachusetts Gaming Commission (Commission or MGC).
First, we welcomed our newest Commissioner, Paul Brodeur, to the Commission. Appointed by Governor Maura Healey,
Commissioner Brodeur brings a wealth of knowledge to our organization. He has already made an impact on our work
and our team, assisting Commissioner Brad Hill with our legislative work, serving as Secretary, and strengthening our
relationships with local communities.
Regulated sports wagering continued to be a priority for the Commission in FY2025, and we are proud to be at the
forefront of this evolving industry. The MGC has taken a leading role in the national discussion on wager limitations,
examining this issue with a focus on our ongoing commitment to consumer protection. Sports wagering continues to
generate resources for the Commonwealth, bringing in $155.64M to the state in FY2025. We will continue listening
to patrons and working with our licensees to ensure sports wagering is regulated in a fair and transparent way in
the Commonwealth.
I am proud to have worked with Commissioner Eileen O’Brien to implement our Seal of Approval program. This newly
designed seal is now mandated to be displayed on the user interfaces of every licensee in Massachusetts, serving as
a clear reminder to players that they are wagering with a safe, regulated operator and not with unsafe, offshore, or
otherwise illegal sportsbooks.
While sports wagering continues to grow, the Commission remains focused on overseeing our three brick-and-mortar
casinos and the live horse racing and simulcasting industries. Our brick-and-mortar casinos generated $340.63M in
state taxes and employed roughly 5,300 people. We also saw standardbred horses compete in 109 races in 2025.
The Community Mitigation Fund (CMF) completed its second year of grants using the block grant formula, and we
are proud to have awarded a record $22.8M to communities and partners impacted by the construction of the casino
facilities. This year my fellow Commissioners and I were able to see the CMF’s grants in action across the state—from
the Boston Chinatown Neighborhood Center and the International Institute of New England to Foxborough’s Marilyn
Rodman Performing Arts Center, and a tour of Holyoke Community College’s MGM Culinary Arts Institute.
The CMF continues to be an important source of funding for impactful projects across the Commonwealth, and I want
to thank Commissioner Brad Hill for his continued leadership of this vital program.
I would like to thank Commissioner Nakisha Skinner for her leadership in overseeing the completion of our latest
diversity study. The study examined participation by minority, women, and veteran-owned businesses and workers
across the sports wagering industry. The findings will guide our ongoing efforts to ensure diversity and inclusion remain
central to the gaming industry in Massachusetts.
Finally, thank you to Governor Maura Healey for entrusting me with the role of Chair after serving in an interim capacity.
Working with not only my fellow Commissioners, but the entire team here is an honor and a privilege that I do not take
lightly. It is my hope that the following report will show you the hard work done by the Massachusetts Gaming Commission.

ABOUT THE
MASSACHUSETTS
GAMING COMMISSION
The Massachusetts Gaming Commission consists of a five-member independent body, an Executive Director
and approximately 130 staff across our Boston headquarters and the three casino properties.
The Massachusetts Gaming Commission was created through Chapter 194 of the Acts of 2011 (The
Gaming Act) to regulate the establishment of new gaming facilities in the Commonwealth, including the
implementation and licensing process. The Gaming Act divided the Commonwealth into three geographic
regions and allowed for one resort casino per region.
Region A
Suffolk, Middlesex, Essex, Norfolk, and Worcester counties
Region B
Hampshire, Hampden, Franklin, and Berkshire counties
Region C
Bristol, Plymouth, Nantucket, Dukes, and Barnstable counties
In 2022, the Massachusetts Gaming Commission was tasked with overseeing the implementation and
promulgation of sports wagering in the Commonwealth, as prescribed by Chapter 173 of the Acts of 2022
(Sports Wagering Act).
The Sports Wagering Act created three categories of sports wagering licensees: Category 1 for licensed
casinos, Category 2 for racetracks and/or simulcast centers, and Category 3 for online mobile operators.
The Commission is directed to determine eligibility for sports wagering licenses.
The costs for the operation of the Massachusetts Gaming Commission is paid solely by the gaming industry
and not by Massachusetts tax dollars.

MISSION STATEMENT
AND CORE VALUES
Mission Statement
The mission of the Massachusetts Gaming Commission is to regulate the legal casino, horse racing,
and sports wagering industries with integrity and transparency. Our goal is to maximize consumer
protections, minimize negative impacts, and promote responsible gaming through regulation, research,
and informed policy making. The Commission endeavors to create a fair and safe gaming environment
in the Commonwealth while fostering economic benefits and a diverse workforce for its licensees,
residents, and businesses.
Core Values
The commissioners and all employees of the Massachusetts Gaming Commission are committed to
public service and a set of core values that will underpin and guide our work.
The MGC and its employees’ value:
• Working collaboratively with our licensees, stakeholders, and the public for the benefit of all
Massachusetts residents.
• Fostering an inclusive culture at the Commission and among our partners in the Massachusetts
gaming, horse racing, and sports wagering industry that reflects the diversity of the Commonwealth.
• Maintaining the integrity of the Commission’s work to promote objective and thorough regulation,
enforcement, and compliance.
• Ensuring transparency in the regulation and oversight of the industries we oversee.
• Holding the Commission and its employees to the highest level of ethical conduct through a commitment
to the law and our enhanced code of ethics.
• Serving the public with exemplary professionalism and customer service for all whom we come in contact.
• Promoting research and evidence-based best practices to assess the impact of expanded gaming,
horse racing, and sports wagering to prevent and mitigate gambling-related harms.
• A commitment to promoting responsible gaming to minimize potential gambling-related harm to
individuals and the community.

MGC RESPONSIBLE
GAMING PROGRAMS
GAMESENSE
GameSense is an innovative responsible gaming program that
encourages casino patrons who chose to gamble to adopt and/or
maintain positive behaviors and attitudes that reduce the risk of
gambling-related harm. The program offers tips on responsible
gaming, engaging tools, and advice and referrals to reduce harm.
GameSense Info Centers are located on-site at all Massachusetts
casinos and staffed by trained GameSense Advisors during casino
operating hours.
PLAYMYWAY
PlayMyWay is a first-of-its-kind budget-setting tool for slot machine
play at Encore Boston Harbor, Plainridge Park Casino, and MGM
Springfield. This tool allows players the ability to monitor the amount
of money they spend on electronic gaming machines.
RESEARCH AGENDA
The MGC established the nation’s most comprehensive research
program to study, assess and prepare for the social and economic
impacts of casino gambling. The MGC is committed to an open and
transparent research program, ensuring easy and convenient access
to its findings, analysis, and data.
VOLUNTARY SELF-EXCLUSION PROGRAM (VSE)
The Voluntary Self-Exclusion Program (VSE) is a statewide program
that allows patrons to voluntarily exclude themselves from casino
gambling, sports wagering, or both, for a predetermined amount of
time. By enrolling in the program, patrons agree to forfeit or suspend all
rewards points and will automatically be removed from marketing lists.

SEAL OF APPROVAL
The MGC developed and adopted an official seal of approval, which all
licensed sports betting operators in the state are required to display
on their digital platforms. The seal is intended to serve as a clear
signal to consumers that they are accessing a sports betting platform
licensed and regulated by the MGC. By prominently featuring this seal,
customers can easily distinguish between legal, licensed operators,
and unsafe, illegal alternatives.
FAIR DEAL
Fair Deal is a program created by the MGC to help ensure that all
gaming activity is fair and ethical. Fair Deal highlights that regulatory
compliance is critical to the public’s confidence in the Massachusetts
Gaming industry. Any person with concerns or personal knowledge
of unethical behavior or suspected violations of the Massachusetts
gaming law or regulations can submit a tip on our website to be
reviewed by Commission staff. Appropriate next steps, including
possible regulatory enforcement action, will occur.
PLAY MANAGEMENT
Through regulation (205 CMR 255: Play Management) the MGC
requires sports wagering operators in the Commonwealth to maintain
play management programs, which allow individuals who have a
sports wagering account to set personal limitations regarding their
sports wagering. Play Management programs are intended to offer
individuals a means to restrict gambling behavior and to increase
informed player choice. Play Management is just another example
of the MGC’s commitment to responsible gaming and player health.

FY2025 HIGHLIGHT:
COMMUNITY
MITIGATION FUND
The Gaming Act created the Community Mitigation Fund (CMF) to support communities and governmental entities in
offsetting impact costs related to the construction and operation of gaming facilities by allocating 6.5% of the gross
gaming tax revenue to this fund.
Since 2015, the MGC has granted approximately $79.5 million in funding to advance needs in transportation, community
planning, workforce development, and public safety initiatives for eligible municipalities and government entities
located in the vicinity of the casinos.
In the fall of 2023 the structure of the Community Mitigation Fund was changed to facilitate the distribution of funds to
communities and regional agencies. The new Block Grant structure enabled communities and other eligible agencies to
more easily apply for funding.
Below is a highlight of Community Mitigation Funds in each region. Commissioners had the chance to visit
these projects in FY2025 and see firsthand the impact of these funds in the community.
a
REGION A
(Cities/Towns surrounding Encore Boston Harbor)
Metro Boston Regional Hospitality Consortium
The Metro North Regional Employment Board (d/b/a MassHire Metro North Workforce Board) developed the Metro
Boston Regional Hospitality Consortium. This program is aimed at addressing workforce needs of the hospitality sector
impacted by Encore Boston Harbor and serves 1,500 local residents in receiving workforce training. The MassHire Metro
North Workforce Board subcontracts with various institutions in surrounding communities to best serve the needs of
their students.
This program has been awarded $3.9M in CMF funds since 2017, including $750,000 in the FY2025 grant cycle.

FY2025
FINANCIAL REPORT
MGC’s Division of Administration and Finance (A&F), led by the Chief Financial and Accounting Officer, is responsible
for the strict oversight of casino, sports wagering, and fantasy sports wagering revenues and the collection of taxes due
to the Commonwealth from those operations. The taxes and assessments are the following:
Casino Taxes on Gross Gaming Revenues:
• Category 2 licensee is 49%
• Category 1 licensees is 25%
Sports Wagering Taxes on Adjusted Gross Sports Wagering Receipts:
• Category 1 licensee (in-person betting) 15%
• Category 2 licensee (in-person betting) 15%
• Category 3 licensee (tethered and untethered mobile betting) 20%
Daily Fantasy Sports Taxes on Adjusted Gross Fantasy Wagering Receipts:
• 15% (Daily Fantasy Sports is regulated by the Massachusetts Attorney General’s Office)
The Division is also in charge of completing all financial transactions and coordinating with other functional areas to
complete administrative functions including, but not limited to, accounting, budgeting, contracting, and revenue collection.
CASINO GAMING REVENUES AND TAX COLLECTIONS
MGM Springfield reported $277.1M in gross gaming revenue, which generated $69.28M in taxes for the Commonwealth.
Encore Boston Harbor reported $740.69M in gross gaming revenue, which generated $185.17M in taxes. The total of
taxes for Category 1 licensees amounted to $254.45M.
In its tenth year of operation, the Category 2 licensee, Plainridge Park Casino, reported $175.87M in gross gaming
revenues, which generated $70.35M in tax dollars to local aid and $15.83M in assessments to the Race Horse
Development Fund for a total of $86.18M in taxes.
Combined, gaming licensees generated $1.19 billion in gross gaming revenue (GGR) and contributed $340.63M to
the Commonwealth for FY2025.

SPORTS WAGERING REVENUES AND TAX COLLECTIONS
Sports Wagering was legalized in the Commonwealth in August of 2022. The Commission authorized in-person betting
to begin in late January 2023. For Fiscal Year 2025, the numbers reported for in-person sportsbooks reflect the period
from July 2024 through June 2025. In its second full year of operation in Sports Wagering, Plainridge Park Casino’s
sportsbook reported $2.55M in Adjusted Gross Sports Wagering Receipts (AGSWR), resulting in $383.3K in taxes for the
Commonwealth. MGM’s sportsbook reported $177.1M in AGSWR resulting in $26.6K in taxes for FY2025. Encore Boston
Harbor’s sportsbook reported $3.24M in AGSWR resulting in $486.26K in taxes.
Mobile sports wagering was authorized by the Commission to begin in March of 2023. Numbers reported below reflect
the time period of July of 2024 through June of 2025. Category three operators (mobile sports wagering operators)
generated $748.9M in AGSWR which resulted in $149.78M in taxes to the Commonwealth.
Daily Fantasy Sports Wagering (DFS) was legalized in the Commonwealth in August of 2022. Taxes on DFS started
accumulating on the date of the passage of the legislation. While the Commission is not responsible for regulation
DFS, it is responsible for tax collections. For the period of July of 2024 through June of 2025 DFS operators generated
$33.08M in AGFWR generating $4.96M in taxes for the Commonwealth.
Combined, sports wagering licensees generated $787.96 million in adjusted gross wagering revenues and
contributed $155.64M to the Commonwealth for FY2025.

COMMUNITY AFFAIRS
Community Mitigation Fund Grant Program
The Community Mitigation Fund (CMF) receives monies from the taxes on gross gaming revenues and is designed
to address impacts that result from the construction and operation of casinos. It may also be used for planning to
determine how to achieve further benefits from a facility.
The Division of Community Affairs reviews requests for mitigation dollars and makes recommendations to the
Commission on the award of grants from the Community Mitigation Fund. These recommendations are developed
through an intensive process involving analysis by many MGC staff members.
During FY2025, the Community Affairs Division made major modifications to the CMF program to improve the utilization
of the funds. The modifications resulted in the development of the Municipal Block Grant Program and the Regional
Agency Grant Program. The modifications included: enhanced guidance on the identification of casino-related impacts
and the selection of mitigation projects; the development of a grant distribution formula to equitably distribute funds to
eligible communities; and the development of guidance for the Regional Agency Grant Program.
The Municipal Block Grant Program provided funds for eligible municipalities to mitigate casino-related impacts in the
areas of public safety, community planning, transportation, and gambling harm reduction. The Regional Agency Grant
Program funded projects to be carried out by regional agencies in the areas of workforce development, public safety,
and regional planning.

wagering operators, focusing on areas such as information security, change management, testing, and reporting.
By ensuring that all gaming technologies and systems comply with state regulations and standards, the GTCU plays
a crucial role in maintaining the integrity and security of Massachusetts’s gaming environment.
Casino Gaming
Currently, there are 5,260 active electronic gaming devices (EGDs) operating across the three casinos in Massachusetts.
The GTCU continuously monitors these devices to ensure compliance and integrity. The team is also responsible for
issuing certifications and permits for EGDs within the state. In FY2025, 561 certification letters were provided by Gaming
Laboratories International (GLI), and 150 certification letters were provided by BMM Testlabs (BMM). To maintain
compliance, the MGC Central Monitoring System (CMS) verifies software signatures for licensees on a daily basis.
Additionally, each Certified Independent Test Lab (CITL) undergoes an annual review to ensure strict adherence to the
standards set forth by the MGC.
Issuing revocations typically begins with a notification from the CITL when an issue is identified with a specific piece of
gaming software, detailing the severity and nature of the issue. The GTCU team reviews each notification to verify if the
software is active in Massachusetts. A notification is then sent to each property, including software information, removal
date, and, if any, the affected EGDs. During FY2025, 47 revocations were issued based on recommendations from CITLs.
The GTCU is also responsible for evaluating, inspecting, and investigating EGDs and related equipment across
Massachusetts. Integration and interoperability testing ensures that EGDs effectively communicate with the MGC’s CMS
as well as the house systems of our licensees. Through systematic testing, the team upholds the reporting, compliance,
and alerting standards set by the MGC. Additionally, specific EGD hardware and cabinets are tested at the request of
licensees in response to the popularity of certain games or themes.

Investigations & Enforcement Bureau/Division of Licensing
The Investigations and Enforcement Bureau (IEB) is comprised of 1) the Massachusetts State Police (MSP)
Gaming Enforcement Unit (GEU), which maintains a 24/7 presence at all three licensed gaming establishments;
2) the Financial Investigations Division, a team of civilian financial investigators, who conduct suitability investigations;
3) the Gaming Agents Division, comprised of civilian agents who maintain a 24/7 presence in the three licensed gaming
establishments, and are charged with providing regulatory oversight and on-site monitoring of the establishments; and
4) the Chief Enforcement Counsel’s Office (CEC), comprised of a team of a paralegal/intake officer and attorneys, whose
duties include legal review of investigations and representing the IEB in licensing and enforcement actions initiated by
the IEB. The Director of the IEB also oversees the Division of Licensing, which administers the licensing and registration
functions on behalf of the Commission.
The IEB conducts probity investigations to determine the suitability of gaming and sports wagering operators, employees,
and vendors. Statute and regulation define thresholds, standards, and procedures for licensing and registration.
There are five types of employee licensure: Key Gaming Employee—Executive; Key Gaming Employee—Standard; Gaming
Employee; Key Sports Wagering Employee—Executive; and Key Sports Wagering Employee—Standard. If an employee is
not classified by regulation, they must register as a Gaming Service Employee, unless exempted from classification by
the Commission.
Similarly, there are three levels of casino vendor licensure and registration: Gaming Vendor—Primary; Gaming Vendor—
Secondary; and Non-Gaming Vendor. For sports wagering, there are Sports Wagering Vendors; Sports Wagering
Registrants; and Non-Sports Wagering Vendors. Each credentialed employee or vendor must submit to a background
check. The depth of all background checks are commensurate with the level of licensure/registration.

FY2025 Research Findings Highlights:
• W hile the population prevalence of people at-risk of or experiencing problem gambling did not increase after casino
introduction, a greater proportion of casino revenue from these groups has increased, 74% in 2013, versus 90% in
2022. This group of people represents 9.9% of the population in Massachusetts.(Social and Economic Impacts of
Casino Introduction to Massachusetts).
• T he introduction of casinos to Massachusetts has significantly increased overall economic activity and employment
in the state, particularly in regions proximate to the casinos. The casino tax on gross gaming revenue provides
hundreds of millions in tax revenue that benefits state and local governments. While the economic gains outweigh
losses, there are notable outflows of casino profits out of state and negative impacts on other industries. (Social and
Economic Impacts of Casino Introduction to Massachusetts).
• T here was a consistent and significant increase in gambling intensity in people who gamble monthly. Gambling
intensity, measured by the number of formats people who gamble monthly participate in and the number of days
gambled in the last year, has risen steadily. The mean number of gambling formats increased from 3.6 in 2014, to 4.6
in 2022, and to 6 in 2023. Similarly, the mean number of days gambled per year increased from 85.3 in 2014, to 103 in
2022, to 112 in 2023. (Gambling and Problem Gambling in Massachusetts: Results of Three Online Panel Surveys).
• T here have been increases in gambling-related harms among people who gamble regularly. This includes
recreational gambling decreasing while harmful gambling increases, as well as increases in gambling-related
harms, as between 2014 and 2023, the only harm that did not increase among those who gambled regularly was
health-related harms. (Gambling and Problem Gambling in Massachusetts: Results of Three Online Panel Surveys).
• S pectrum Gaming Group, who prepared the report Feasibility Study: Prospective Sports Wagering Kiosks in
Massachusetts, recommended not implementing sports wagering kiosks in Massachusetts, as the projected
economic benefits for both potential kiosk hosts and the Commonwealth are minimal, while there is an increased
risk for potential social harms. Although some small businesses might see marginal gains, these are unlikely to
outweigh the financial, social, and administrative costs required to support such a program and ensure its viability.

Social and Economic Impacts of Casino Introduction to Massachusetts
October 24, 2024
The introduction of casinos to Massachusetts has significantly increased overall economic activity
and employment in the state, particularly in regions proximate to the casinos. The casino tax on
gross gaming revenue provides hundreds of millions in tax revenue that benefits state and local
governments. While the economic gains outweigh losses, there are notable outflows of casino
profits out of state and negative impacts on other industries. Casinos have not increased the
prevalence of problem gambling, likely attributable to the ready availability of casino gambling
in neighboring states. However, much of the additional economic activity comes from spending
from people at risk of or experiencing gambling problems, who are estimated to account for 90%
of casino revenue in 2021/2022. While the actual population prevalence of problem and at-risk
gambling did not increase, the revenue drawn from this vulnerable segment of the population
likely has increased.

RESPONSIBLE GAMING INITIATIVES
Since its inception, the MGC has developed, implemented, refined, and adapted its responsible gaming strategy to
respond to the needs of all patrons. Responsible gaming is a core priority and central to the MGC mission. Objectives
of the program are to 1) promote positive play, 2) reduce gambling related harms, and 3) support the development of
a responsible gaming focused and capable workforce.
The Voluntary Self-Exclusion (VSE) Program assists patrons who recognize that they have experienced a loss of
control over their gambling and wish to invoke external controls. People enroll in the program for one, three, or five-
year terms and are prohibited from wagering at Massachusetts casinos or sports wagering mobile platforms. If they do,
any gambling winnings are confiscated and transferred to the Gaming Revenue Fund. The MGC’s VSE process utilizes an
engaged approach, ensuring that enrollees are offered additional assistance, responded to in a respectful, timely and
discreet manner, and feel supported. In FY2025, a total of 746 persons enrolled in the VSE program, a 13% increase from
FY24. Remote VSE was implemented on January 4, 2021 in response to the COVID pandemic and in FY2025 there were
334 remote VSE enrollments, a 19% increase from FY24. On January 23, 2023, MGC began accepting VSE enrollments
for Sports Wagering. As of June 30, 2025, there are 2,052 people enrolled in the Gaming VSE and 794 people enrolled in
the Sports Wagering VSE.
GameSense is an innovative responsible gaming program based at Massachusetts casinos and available virtually
through LiveChat with a goal of promoting positive play behaviors and attitudes that reduce the risk of gambling-related
harm. In furtherance of the statute, which calls for an on-site player protection program, MGC licensed the GameSense
brand from the British Columbia Lottery Corporation in 2015 and drew upon the experience of the Massachusetts
Council on Gaming and Health staff to operate the program at all three licensee properties.
Most people who visit Massachusetts casinos engage in safe levels of gambling behavior, also known as positive play.
Based at each MA casino, GameSense Information Centers are staffed by trained GameSense Advisors who offer a range
of information, resources and tools with a goal of increasing gambling literacy and reducing gambling related harms. In
FY2025, GameSense Advisors engaged with over 86,800 casino and sports wagering patrons and employees to deliver
information about responsible gaming and problem gambling. To put a finer point on this, of these interactions 29% were
about GameSense generally, 11% were about setting a budget, 16% were about PlayMyWay, 10% were about positive
play strategies, and 13% were about debunking odds and myths. In addition, GameSense Advisors provide training to
staff at all three Massachusetts casinos with a goal to increase their knowledge about responsible gaming and spur
referrals to the GameSense program. In FY2025, 986 people were trained at 114 training events.
In 2021, the MGC launched the first 24-hour online chat dedicated to responsible gaming in the U.S. Chat users connect
directly with GameSense Advisors to enroll in the Voluntary Self-Exclusion program, connect with help, learn tips on
responsible gaming and rules of the games. LiveChat is accessible on GameSense’s website, GameSenseMA.com. In
FY23, GameSenseMA.com was updated to include dedicated responsible gaming information about sports wagering,
debunking gambling myths and misconceptions, and educating players on how games, odds and probabilities work. In
FY2025, GameSense Advisors engaged in 1,738 chats on the GAM Line and LiveChat. Of these engagements 69% were
about VSE information, 18% were about sports wagering application information, 9% were about casino information,
and 4% were about information on the games.

PlayMyWay (PMW) is a first in the nation budgeting tool designed to allow players the ability to set a budget and
monitor the amount of money they spend on electronic gaming machines. PMW was first launched at Plainridge Park
Casino in 2016 followed by MGM Springfield in 2018. On September 12, 2022, PMW became available at Encore Boston
Harbor, meaning that it’s available on all slots and electronic gaming machines in Massachusetts. Once enrolled, a
player receives automatic notifications when approaching their daily, weekly, or monthly budget. They may then choose
to stop at any point or keep playing. PMW allows enrollment/unenrollment and budget adjustments at any time. As of
June 30, 2025 there were 62,102 players actively enrolled in PlayMyWay with an average un-enrollment rate of 12.6%.
Responsible Gaming tools on Sports Wagering platforms are offered to all sports wagering players on mobile
applications and digital websites. As required by regulation, sports wagering operators offer play management tools
similar to PMW that enable sports wagering players to set limits on deposits into sports wagering account, specified
dollar amount of a wager, and cumulative dollar amount wagered.
